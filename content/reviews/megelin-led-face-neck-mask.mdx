## How LED Photobiomodulation Works

LEDs are semiconductor light sources. In red and near-infrared photobiomodulation research, proposed light–tissue interactions involve endogenous photoacceptors and downstream signaling. Cytochrome c oxidase is **one proposed target**, not the only established explanation for every wavelength, tissue or response. Changes in mitochondrial activity, ATP, reactive oxygen species or nitric oxide discussed in experimental research must not be recast as measured effects of this mask.<Citation number={2} />

Mechanistic plausibility, a laboratory observation and a human clinical outcome answer different questions. A cellular signal does not by itself demonstrate a visible wrinkle improvement, and a trial using a particular lamp or mask does not validate every device described as red-light therapy. No product-specific collagen, ATP or mitochondrial experiment is claimed here.

Our [How LED Face Masks Work](/science/how-led-masks-work) explains the sequence from light generation to tissue interactions. [Red vs Near-Infrared Light](/science/red-vs-near-infrared) covers why visibility and optical behavior should not be confused with proven benefit.

## Seven-Color Technology: What Is Known and What Remains Unclear

The supplied seller information describes seven light-color settings and an overall **420–850 nm** wavelength span. It does not provide an independently verified spectral peak, bandwidth or power distribution for each mode. An overall span is not a mode map and does not establish continuous wavelength tuning.

Different spectral regions interact differently with tissue absorbers and scattering structures. A label such as blue, red or near-infrared cannot independently establish a biological target, appropriate exposure or human benefit. Seven settings must not be presented as seven independently proven treatments.

## Irradiance, Fluence and Treatment Time

Wavelength describes spectral position. **Irradiance** describes incident optical power per unit area at a stated location, commonly in mW/cm². Session **radiant exposure**, often called fluence in consumer discussions, describes energy per unit area, commonly in J/cm². Time, measurement distance, operating mode and spatial distribution are all part of the exposure description.

For stable continuous illumination, multiplying irradiance in W/cm² by exposure time in seconds gives incident radiant exposure in J/cm². This relationship does not supply missing measurements, establish absorbed tissue dose, or prove that two devices with the same calculated exposure produce the same response. Pulsed or combined modes require their actual time-dependent output to be characterized.

The supplied schedule is **10–20 minutes per session, 3–4 times weekly, for 4–6 weeks**. This is the seller's reported guidance, not a ProofWell treatment prescription or promise of improvement after that period. Without verified irradiance, we cannot calculate this model's dose from the session length or recommend extending it.

Our [Irradiance vs Fluence](/science/irradiance-vs-fluence) guide explains why LED count, a broad wavelength range and a long session are insufficient ways to rank devices. This mask's **288 face LEDs and 198 neck LEDs** remain seller-listed counts, not evidence of a high or beneficial dose.
